T109|x|THE SCHEDULER'S PLAYHEAD GOES STALE WHENEVER A VIEWER IS BEING SERVED WELL, AND EVERY CONSUMER OF IT DEGRADES TOGETHER. `note_playhead` only fires on an interactive `Submit`, i.e. on a segment that reached the transcode scheduler — a cache MISS. A viewer whose buffer is warm takes fast cache hits (`hit_path="fast"`) and coalesced hits (`hit_path="coalesced"`), and neither reaches the scheduler at all, so the last reading for that stream is wherever it last missed. The better the prefetch, the more stale the number. That number is not decorative: `lookahead_distance` is read by `next_to_dispatch` (which speculative job is most urgent), by `is_stale` (which is dropped), by `queue_or_refuse` (which is evicted), and by `pharos_transcode_queue_distance` (the histogram an operator reads to decide whether prefetch depth is tuned too deep). All four are wrong in the same direction at once, and the error is proportional to how well the system is working — a viewer at segment 140 whose playhead still reads 120 has every one of its guesses ranked twenty segments further out than it is, so its NEXT genuine miss queues behind another stream's shallower work. SHAPE OF THE FIX (needs its own design pass, deliberately not folded into the 006 final-review wave): a new scheduler message, `SchedMsg::Playhead { stream, segment }`, sent by `segment_bytes_keyed` on BOTH hit paths when `class == Interactive`, handled by the same `note_playhead` the Submit arm calls. It cannot ride on `Submit` — there is no job — and it must not be inferred inside the scheduler, which never hears about a hit. Two things need deciding rather than assuming. (a) COST: a hit is the common case, so this adds an mpsc send per served segment; the actor is already fed at segment rate by submissions and completions, and the send is fire-and-forget (`try_send`, dropping on a full inbox — a lost playhead update is exactly as bad as today's behaviour and never worse), but it should be measured before it ships. (b) MONOTONICITY: a hit for segment N arriving after a miss for N+2 (parallel fetches during a seek) must not drag the playhead backwards; `note_playhead` currently overwrites unconditionally, which is correct for a submission and wrong for an out-of-order hit. Either the hit path takes a max against the current reading, or the message carries enough to be ordered. Note that a MAX is not obviously right either — a backward seek genuinely moves the playhead back, and today only a miss (which a seek always produces) can express that. Guards to add with it: a test that a warm viewer's playhead advances on cache hits, and one that an out-of-order hit does not move it backwards. Found by the whole-branch review of 006 (`feat/self-tuning-playback`), where the fix's blast radius across four consumers is what argued for a separate pass rather than a follow-up patch. RESOLVED: `SchedMsg::Playhead` + `TranscodeScheduler::note_playhead`, sent from both hit paths in `segment_bytes_keyed` for `Interactive` requests on a named stream. (a) COST — synchronous fire-and-forget `try_send`, dropping on a full inbox and counting the drop (`pharos_transcode_playhead_dropped_total`), so the path holding a viewer's segment never waits on the actor; measured end-to-end, interleaved A/B against the same fast-hit path with and without a scheduler attached, on a 2 MiB cached segment: +2.1-3.1 us on a ~114 us hit (1.9-2.7%), the `try_send` itself ~50 ns, and a production hit reads a multi-MB segment off a network PVC so the real fraction is smaller. (b) MONOTONICITY — `PlayheadMotion`: a submission may move a reading ANYWHERE (that is how a backward seek is expressed), a hit may only ADVANCE it (a hit says the viewer REACHED that segment, never that they went back, and hits land in completion order, not request order). One rule, no ordering metadata. What it does NOT cover, deliberately: a rewind landing on a segment still in cache is a HIT, so the reading stays where the viewer was — which is what happens today too, and closing it needs the request's arrival instant carried on BOTH messages so the map is written in request order. A hit also SEEDS a stream with no reading, closing the re-watch gap `PlayheadSeed` documents. Guarded by V58 and by `a_hit_may_advance_a_playhead_but_never_drag_it_backwards`, `a_playhead_update_is_dropped_rather_than_stalling_the_viewer_serving_it`, `a_warm_viewers_playhead_advances_on_cache_hits`, `a_late_hit_behind_the_viewer_does_not_drag_the_playhead_back`, `a_coalesced_hit_moves_the_joiners_playhead_and_not_the_drivers`.
